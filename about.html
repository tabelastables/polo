Our Heritage

Born of Royal Patronage

The Punjab Polo Club was founded in October 1898 at the personal behest of His Highness Maharaja Rajinder Singh of Patiala, himself a nine-goal player who had competed at the highest levels in England. He donated 200 acres of land outside Ludhiana, commissioning the Scottish architect James Strachan to design the original pavilion — its teak beams and arched verandahs still standing today.

Through the decades of colonial India, independence, partition, and modernity, the club has endured as a beacon of sporting excellence and social distinction. Our grounds have hosted the Viceroy's Cup, the British Army championships, and in recent decades, the Indian Open — one of the world's premier high-goal tournaments.

Today the club counts among its members industrialists, royalty, diplomats, and athletes from across the globe — all united by the thundering hooves and green turf of the maidan.

Through the Decades

A Timeline of Distinction

1898

Foundation

Maharaja Rajinder Singh of Patiala establishes the Punjab Polo Club. The original pavilion — designed by James Strachan — is completed within twelve months. The first fixture is played on 4th November 1898.

1912

The Viceroy's Cup

The club hosts the inaugural Viceroy's Cup, attracting teams from across British India and the British Army. PPC's own team, the Golden Lances, claim the trophy — beginning a dynasty that will last six consecutive years.

1947

Independence & Resilience

Through the turbulence of Partition, the club continues to operate — one of the few institutions to emerge from 1947 largely intact. Membership transitions from colonial to Indian ownership. The club formally incorporates as a trust.

1968

The Grand Pavilion

A major renovation programme replaces the ageing colonial structure with the iconic Grand Pavilion — a blend of Mughal and modernist architecture that still serves as the club's social heart today.

1993

Championship Maidan B

The club expands with a second international-standard maidan, allowing simultaneous tournaments. Argentine polo pony breeding is introduced to the club's stables — transforming the quality of play.

2009

The Indian Open

Punjab Polo Club is selected as the permanent home of the Indian Open Polo Championship — a 22-goal tournament that now attracts the world's finest players each February.

2024

Centennial Renovations

Ahead of the 2028 centenary, a ₹45-crore renovation programme is launched — restoring the original pavilion, modernising stable facilities, and establishing the PPC Junior Academy for the next generation.

The Grounds

World-Class Facilities

Championship Maidan A

Our flagship 300-yard turf ground — maintained to HURLINGHAM POLO ASSOCIATION specifications. Hosts all major tournaments and the Indian Open. Capacity: 8,000 spectators.

Championship Maidan B

The second full-size ground, ideal for mid-week fixtures, coaching clinics, and practice matches. Features a dedicated stick-and-ball warmup area.

The Royal Stables

80 purpose-built loose-boxes with automated watering, climate control, and individual tack rooms. Home to our 48 club ponies of Argentine and Marwari lineage, cared for by 22 full-time grooms.

The Maharaja Dining Room

An award-winning restaurant with panoramic maidan views. Our Chef de Cuisine trained at the Ritz Paris. Serves contemporary Punjabi cuisine alongside a curated international wine list.

The Grand Pavilion

Our signature event space — 1,200 sq m of heritage architecture accommodating up to 600 guests for weddings, galas, and corporate events. Booked one year in advance on average.

Members' Lounge & Spa

A private sanctuary for members — billiards room, cigar lounge, whisky library, and an Ayurvedic spa offering treatments to riders and their guests alike.
